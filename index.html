Phase 1 · Complete
Launch & Operational Readiness
Program launched on time — OH/TX/UT on 3/17/2026, WA on 4/8/2026. Members are being contacted at expected rates. The program is operating as designed.
Phase 2 · Ongoing
Reach & Response
Outreach continues across all 4 markets — new members are enrolling every week. Reach & Response runs in parallel with Deep Engagement: members who enrolled in Month 1 are already in barrier work while new members are still being brought in.
What this data tells us "Are we reaching the right people?" Members are responding across all four markets. 14.2% of members reached are enrolling — still well above the 10% contractual minimum — even after we scaled outreach to more than 41,800 members. Washington leads at 14.6%, with Ohio (14.4%), Utah (13.9%), and Texas (13.7%) all clustered tightly above the floor. Every enrolled member moves into needs-based conversation with a dedicated care team member within the first engagement.
Talking Points — Reach & Response
Conversion rate — "DSNP members are among the most complex and hardest-to-reach populations in managed care. Even after tripling our outreach base, more than 1 in 8 members reached are choosing to engage — and stay engaged — well above our 10% commitment. This population wants this support; they just haven't had access to it."
Washington leads at 14.6%, all 4 markets above the 10% floor — "Washington launched weeks later than the other markets, serves the population Molina specifically flagged as the hardest to reach, and has now enrolled 1,969 members — while leading the program on conversion at 14.6%. We have now reached nearly every member in the Washington eligibility file. The barriers are real — but members are responding in every market, with every cohort."

Phase 3 · Now Active
Deep Engagement & Barrier Visibility
Sustained engagement is surfacing barriers that standard outreach doesn't identify — social determinants, behavioral health needs, and care access gaps that exist in the quiet moments between appointments. This is why members aren't connecting to care.
What this data tells us "Are we learning anything meaningful that can change outcomes?" Yes. 6,012 members have completed PRAPARE SDOH screenings — 95–97% across all 4 markets identify at least one social need, averaging ~3.1 needs per member. Economic instability is the primary barrier in every market. PHQ-4 (7,974 screenings) and UCLA-3 (8,052 screenings) baselines are now established across all markets, creating the measurement foundation for demonstrating clinical change over time. This is the data that drives everything downstream.
Talking Points — Deep Engagement & Barrier Visibility
SDOH screening completion (~95% in TX, UT, WA) — "We typically see around 70% completion on SDOH assessments. We're running at 95% across three of your four markets. That reflects two things: genuine trust between your members and our care team, and the fact that these members want to talk about what's getting in their way. You can't solve barriers you can't see — now we can see them, at scale."
PHQ-4 baselines established — "We've documented mental health baselines across hundreds of members. This creates the measurement foundation to demonstrate improvement in HOS emotional health outcomes over time. You can't move a measure you haven't first established."
UCLA-3 loneliness baselines — "Social isolation is one of the least visible drivers of low member experience scores — and one of the hardest things for a plan to measure on its own. We now have baseline loneliness scores for enrolled members. Consistent engagement directly addresses this."
Avg 2.9 social needs per member — "Members are carrying an average of nearly three concurrent social needs. These aren't isolated problems — they're layered barriers that require coordinated navigation, not a single referral. That's exactly what our care team is built to do."

Phases 4–5 · Months 4–12
Activation & Impact
Once barriers are identified, members act — and in Month 6 that action is scaling. Our care team is coordinating appointments across the full range of member needs, and Annual Wellness Visits have become the leading activation type. Here's the activation work happening right now.
What Molina will be measuring "Is this helping our quality, utilization, and STARS goals?" Yes — directly. Our care team has coordinated 394 appointments across the four markets, led by 214 Annual Wellness Visits — the single visit type that most directly supports CAHPS and STARS quality. On top of that, 70 appointments target specific STARS/HEDIS measures — blood pressure control (27), diabetes care (26), and breast & colorectal cancer screening (17). The AWV ramp we projected last cycle has now landed. Star rating movement is a 12–18 month lagging indicator; this appointment activity is the leading indicator building that case now.
